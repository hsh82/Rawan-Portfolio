**What if cashback earned at one store could be spent at another?**

That question is the starting point for **Camran**, an educational e-commerce project combining a multi-merchant loyalty business model with an interactive web prototype. The work explores how independent merchants could participate in a shared rewards network—and how such a service would need to feel from the perspectives of customers, merchants, administrators, checkout operators, and support teams.

<Callout type="tip" title="Project scope: educational MVP">Camran demonstrates product concepts and simulated purchasing, cashback, and wallet experiences. It is **not** a live payment service, bank-connected wallet, or production settlement platform.</Callout>

<Figure src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hsh82/camran/main/public/images/dashbords/cash-back-shop.webp" alt="Camran project visual associated with the merchant cashback experience" caption="Camran — the cashback concept from the merchant side." />

## The problem: rewards locked inside one store

A store-specific cashback scheme can encourage repeat purchases, but it also restricts where accumulated credit has value. For customers, a separate reward balance at every shop becomes fragmented. For independent merchants, running individual loyalty programs creates additional operational and promotional work.

Camran's proposed answer is a **shared loyalty network**: rewards earned through one participating merchant could be redeemed at another. That shift makes cashback a network-level product instead of an isolated store promotion.

The concept also raises practical questions: Who funds a reward? How is redemption coordinated across merchants? What information should a customer see? Which management tools would the operator need? The prototype explores the experience and organizational roles; it does not claim to have solved production financial settlement.

## Five interfaces, one product model

Instead of explaining the business idea through a static presentation alone, the MVP provides role-oriented screens to explore the same service from different operational perspectives.

| Interface | Experience represented in the demo |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Stores, simulated purchases, cashback balance, and activity |
| Merchant | Store information, orders, and promotional or reward scenarios |
| Administrator | Platform-level management and oversight concepts |
| POS | A checkout-oriented view of purchases and rewards |
| Support | A service and customer-assistance perspective |

### Customer experience

The customer interface makes the proposed value visible: discover merchants, follow a shopping journey, and understand how a cashback balance would appear. These are demonstration flows, not verified purchases.

## Implementation choices and engineering trade-offs

The academic project combines business-model analysis with a web-based demonstration built using **Next.js, React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and shadcn/ui**. Its interface builds on the open-source **Shadboard** project rather than beginning with an entirely original dashboard foundation.

| Decision | Benefit for this MVP | Boundary or trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start from Shadboard | Accelerates the creation of multiple dashboard views | Requires adaptation to Camran's workflows and inherits upstream design choices |
| Organize interfaces by role | Makes a multi-sided service easier to understand | Does not prove the underlying permission model is production-ready |
| Demonstrate a shared cashback wallet | Makes the cross-merchant value proposition tangible | Real liabilities, settlement, reconciliation, and fraud prevention remain unresolved |
| Prioritize interactive simulation | Enables product exploration without a live financial integration | Simulated activity cannot establish payment reliability or commercial success |

The business report also considers **WordPress, WooCommerce, and Dokan** as possible commerce components. Their exact integration with the public Next.js demonstration is not independently verified, so they should not be treated as a confirmed production backend.

## Business model: value for both sides

The proposal examines a platform connecting customers and independent merchants. Customers would gain broader opportunities to use rewards; merchants could participate in a collective loyalty experience rather than creating separate systems. The study considers merchant subscriptions, commissions, promotional campaigns, and analytics as **possible revenue streams**, not revenue already earned.

The critical dependency is network participation: cashback becomes more useful only when enough relevant merchants accept it, and participation remains viable only when reward costs and merchant benefits are balanced.

## What was delivered—and what was not

**Delivered for demonstration:** a product concept, business-model research, a navigable multi-role web interface, and simulated purchase, cashback, wallet, campaign, and operational journeys.

**Not demonstrated as production capabilities:** real banking integrations, live payment settlement, regulated financial custody, tested merchant-to-merchant reconciliation, comprehensive identity security, fraud controls, or validated customer-retention improvements.

A credible next stage would be a limited merchant pilot with explicit rules for funding, redemption, reconciliation, security, and support, followed by measurement of actual customer and merchant behavior.

## Outcome

Camran translates a multi-sided loyalty proposal into an inspectable product experience. Its value is in connecting **business-model design, role-based user journeys, and frontend prototyping** while keeping the boundary between an interactive MVP and a financial production platform explicit.
